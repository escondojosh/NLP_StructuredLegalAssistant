ORDINANCE NO. 8327 — IMPORTANT PROVISIONS ONLY
City of Manila
Approved: October 4, 2013

TITLE
An ordinance amending certain provisions of Ordinance No. 8092, known as the Traffic Management Code of the City of Manila, including its schedules of fines and penalties.

PURPOSE
The ordinance amends Manila's Traffic Management Code to regulate traffic, public transport routes, loading/unloading areas, certain uses of public spaces, and related enforcement procedures.

KEY PROVISIONS

1. DEFINITIONS
- Terminal: A station where transport vehicles load or unload passengers or goods.
- The ordinance includes a definition of “awning,” but the source text appears damaged or unclear.

2. LOADING AND UNLOADING
- Public utility vehicle drivers must pick up and unload passengers only at designated loading/unloading zones listed in Appendix VII.

3. CITY AND PROVINCIAL BUSES
- Only city buses with terminals in Manila, in numbers determined and authorized by the Traffic Management Committee, may use Manila's streets and roads.
- Provincial buses covered by the provision may load/unload passengers only at their designated terminals.
- Buses may not use Manila's streets and roads if they do not have a designated terminal in the city.
- The Traffic Management Committee must establish markings or identification to distinguish authorized city buses from provincial buses.

4. PUBLIC UTILITY BUS AND JEEPNEY ROUTES
- Covered buses, jeepneys, mega-taxis, and shuttle vans with valid LTFRB authorization and routes beginning or ending in Manila must provide the Manila Traffic and Parking Bureau (MTPB) a copy of their approved routes and follow those routes.
- The Traffic Management Committee may adjust turning points and terminals, set loading/unloading points, or require passenger interchange terminals as part of an approved traffic-improvement scheme.
- Vehicles-for-hire operating without specific routes must not charge fares above those authorized by the LTFRB.

5. USE OF PUBLIC PLACES
- Streets, sidewalks, avenues, alleys, bridges, parks, and other public places in Manila may not be used for commercial or personal purposes without approval by the City Mayor under authority of the City Council.
- The listed prohibited acts include placing signs, signboards, awnings, or awning posts on or above sidewalks when detached from a business establishment.

6. NON-CONTACT APPREHENSION
- Non-contact apprehension may be used for traffic violations where the only penalty under the Code is a fine.
- The Traffic Management Committee must issue implementing rules and regulations.

7. DISTRIBUTION OF COLLECTED FINES
The ordinance specifies the following incentives from collected fines:
- 20% for the traffic enforcers concerned, distributed through ATM.
- 5% for Traffic Enforcement Units.
- 2% for the section commanders concerned.

FINES AND PENALTIES
The source copy's Appendix VI is partly damaged. The entries below are transcribed from the available text; uncertain amounts or details must be checked against an official copy.

- Driving over a yellow box (Sec. 23):
  • 1st offense: ₱2,000
  • 2nd offense: ₱3,000
  • 3rd and succeeding offenses: ₱4,000 plus impounding

- Violation of loading/unloading zone (Sec. 47):
  • 1st offense: ₱2,000
  • 2nd offense: ₱3,000 plus one day of impounding
  • 3rd and succeeding offenses: amount unclear in the scan (appears to be ₱4,000) plus three days of impounding

- No franchise / not covered by CPC (Sec. 102(D)):
  • 1st offense: ₱2,000
  • 2nd offense: ₱3,000 plus one day of impounding
  • 3rd and succeeding offenses: ₱4,000 plus three days of impounding

- Jaywalking (Sec. 131):
  • 1st offense: ₱200
  • 2nd offense: ₱300
  • 3rd and succeeding offenses: ₱500

- Smoke belching (Sec. 134):
  • 1st offense: ₱2,000
  • 2nd offense: ₱3,000
  • 3rd and succeeding offenses: ₱4,000 plus impounding

- Violation of U-turn zone: the source indicates ₱1,000, but the section reference is unclear.
- Another entry under Sec. 11 is damaged; its violation title and first-offense penalty are illegible. The source lists ₱3,000 for the second offense and ₱4,000 plus impounding for the third and succeeding offenses.

FINAL PROVISIONS
- If any part of the ordinance is declared invalid, the unaffected parts remain in effect (separability clause).
- Inconsistent ordinances, rules, and regulations are amended accordingly.
- The ordinance states that it takes effect immediately.
- The City Council of Manila finally enacted it on September 17, 2013; it was approved by the Mayor on October 4, 2013.
